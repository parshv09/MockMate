# HR Interview Preparation & Behavioral Guidelines

## 1. Professional Self-Introduction Structure
- **Elevator Pitch Model:** Structure around **Present $\to$ Past $\to$ Future** within 90 to 120 seconds.
  - *Present:* Current role, core technical domain (languages/frameworks), and scope of day-to-day impact.
  - *Past:* Relevant engineering background, key milestones, shipped systems, or pivotal academic projects.
  - *Future:* Why this specific team and challenge align naturally with your next professional growth stage.
- **Key Guideline:** Avoid reciting your resume verbatim; emphasize measurable impact, technical ownership, and passion for solving system problems.

## 2. Framing Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths:** Choose hard skills paired with collaborative traits (e.g., deep debugging methodology, distributed system resilience, technical mentorship). Support with concrete data: *"Reduced API response latency by 35% through profile-guided indexing."*
- **Weaknesses:** Name a genuine, non-fatal area of technical or operational growth (e.g., public speaking, over-optimizing before validating user demand). Articulate proactive remediation steps taken and demonstrable progress: *"I noticed I spent excessive time optimizing edge-case code paths, so I implemented strict timeboxing and benchmark-driven optimization criteria."*
- **Avoid:** False weaknesses such as *"I am a perfectionist"* or *"I work too hard."*

## 3. Salary Negotiation Strategy
- **Research & Benchmarking:** Leverage market data bands (Levels.fyi, Radford, Glassdoor) normalized for title, level, and geography.
- **Deflecting Early Numbers:** Politely defer initial compensation inquiries until mutual fit is confirmed: *"I'm currently focused on determining whether this role is the right mutual fit. I am confident the team offers competitive compensation aligned with current market rates."*
- **Total Compensation Mindset:** Evaluate the complete package: base salary, performance bonuses, equity (RSUs/stock options), health benefits, retirement matching, and relocation allowances.
- **Competing Offers:** Present competing offers professionally as external market validation without sounding adversarial.

## 4. Career Trajectory & "Why This Company?"
- **Career Goals:** Differentiate short-term goals (mastering the stack, delivering high-velocity features) from long-term ambitions (technical architecture, staff engineering, team mentorship).
- **"Why Us?" Matrix:** Connect personal values to specific company attributes:
  - *Product & Tech Stack:* Cite specific open-source tools, distributed architectures, or engineering blog posts published by the company.
  - *Business & Mission:* Align with the company's product trajectory, customer pain points, and scale challenges.

## 5. Teamwork, Conflict Resolution & Leadership
- **Cross-Functional Collaboration:** Emphasize alignment with Product Managers, UX Designers, and QA. Bridge technical requirements and business goals smoothly.
- **Constructive Workplace Conflict:** Disagree based on data and technical trade-offs rather than egos. Use proofs of concept (PoC) and architecture decision records (ADRs) to test competing viewpoints; practice "disagree and commit."
- **Leadership Without Authority:** Demonstrate informal technical leadership: driving code reviews, documenting runbooks, unblocking colleagues, and conducting blameless retrospectives.

## 6. Work-Life Balance and Handling Feedback
- **Sustainable Output:** Prioritize work using importance-urgency matrices, set clear sprint expectations, avoid premature scope creep, and communicate bandwidth bottlenecks early.
- **Receptivity to Criticism:** Treat critical feedback as diagnostic input. Separate personal identity from code reviews, ask clarifying questions, and implement preventive measures to prevent recurring issues.
